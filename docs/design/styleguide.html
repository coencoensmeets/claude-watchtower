Principles

Four rules that decide every case this guide does not cover.

Colour means state

Ember, lilac and lime belong to sessions. A colour block on screen is always a session telling you something, never decoration.

Ink is for action

Primary buttons, the selected row and your own messages are ink. If it's ink, you can press it or you wrote it.

Round, then rounder

Controls are pills and circles. Containers get a radius that grows with their size, and nested shapes keep a constant gap.

One loud thing per screen

The session that needs you gets the big block. Everything around it stays quiet: neutral surfaces, small type, no shadows.

Colour

The state palette replaces the seed-generated Material scheme. The four state colours are mid-tones chosen so ink text reads on them in both themes, which means a card looks the same in light and dark.

Shape

Controls are pills and circles. Containers step through five radii by size. When a shape sits inside another, its radius is the outer radius minus the padding, so the gap between them stays even all the way round.

Texture

Diagonal hatching is the only texture. It means "nothing here": a closed session, the unused part of a meter, an empty slot. It never fills something active.

Components

The parts the panel is built from, in the order you meet them on screen.

Buttons

Ink for the one thing the screen exists to do. Soft for secondary. Line for dismiss. Rose only where something ends. Height 52, or 40 inside cards.

Icon buttons

Always a circle: 36, 48 or 64. Hover grows 6%, press shrinks 6%. Every one carries an aria-label.

Filter chips

The count bubble carries the state colour, the chip stays neutral. The selected chip turns ink and its bubble turns lime.

Tabs

A segmented pill for the detail pane's tabs. The selected segment lifts to the surface colour, and slides there from the tab you left.

Spacing

  • 4px base: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 72.
  • 6px between rows, 16px between panes.
  • 24px inside cards and panes.

Surfaces

  • Page, then surface, then surface-2. Three levels and no more.
  • No borders between surfaces; the step in tone is the edge.
  • Use a 1.5px line only on outlined controls and closed things.

Below 900px

  • One pane at a time, as today.
  • Detail tabs move to the floating bar at the bottom.
  • Rows grow to 64px; touch targets never drop below 44px.

Motion

Three kinds of motion, kept apart. Response: things move because you did something. Arrival: something new turned up, and only the new thing moves. Ambient: a session is working or needs you, and its row and header say so while it lasts.

Pulse
Working. A ring grows out of the lamp and fades, an arc orbits the avatar, and a slow sheen crosses the header.
Blink
Needs an answer. The lamp dims to 25% and back, every 1.1s, and the glyph gives a small nudge every few seconds.
Still
Waiting on you. Nothing moves; the colour is enough.

Colour changes use cubic-bezier(.2, 0, 0, 1) at 200ms. Presses give a little (scale .94) and spring back with a slight overshoot; rows lean 3px toward the pointer and their avatar grows. The tab pill slides to the tab you chose. Arrivals decelerate: panes rise in once when the page opens, rows cascade in on the first paint, a new message rises out of its own side, a row that changes state pops its avatar with a ring of the new colour, and the header's new colour grows out of its corner over the old one. A poll that changed nothing moves nothing. With reduced motion on, everything holds still and colour alone carries the state. The rules live in web/styles/motion.css.

Do and don't

✓Do

  • Put the most urgent session in the largest colour block on screen.
  • Keep text on state colours ink, in both themes. Action pills flip from ink to chalk in dark.
  • Shrink inner radii so nested shapes keep an even gap.
  • Pair every big number with a short, plain label below it.
  • Name buttons by what they do: Focus window, End session, Allow once.

✕Don't

  • Use a state colour for decoration, branding or emphasis.
  • Add gradients, drop shadows on cards, or borders between surfaces.
  • Animate anything that did not change: a repaint is not news.
  • Give square corners to anything you can click.
  • Use hatching on anything that is still running.
